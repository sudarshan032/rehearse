## How projects are judged
Four questions, weighted, and fixed before the challenge starts.
[30%]
Does it solve a real problem in your work?
A specific problem, a clear user, and a tool that person would keep using.
[30%]
Was Wispr used meaningfully?
Flow is part of the build and the workflow, and your demo and usage card show it.
[25%]
Does it work?
A live link the judges can open and use.
[15%]
Is the demo clear?
A two-minute video anyone can follow.
The Most Wispr usage and Social prizes are ranked on their own. Wispr usage goes by words dictated during the challenge, and Social by how clearly a post shows a real workflow.
How we keep it fair
- 01The criteria and weights above are fixed before the challenge starts.
- 02Every project is scored by multiple judges, and your final score is the average.
- 03Every judge's score for every project is public on the leaderboard.
- 04Every project gets written comments from the judges on how it could improve.
- 05Judges do not score a project they have a connection to.
- 06Scores are published on Wednesday 4 November, before winners are confirmed. If you think something was missed, you can raise it until the end of Thursday 5 November and you will get a written reply.
- 07Winners' Wispr accounts are verified with Wispr before prizes are sent.
Follow it live
The leaderboard is live. It has three views: Projects, with every judge's score and comments, Wispr usage, with words dictated during the challenge, and Social, with everyone's posts.
